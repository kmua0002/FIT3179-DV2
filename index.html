Pokédex Expansion

The Pokédex kept growing

Every generation expanded the roster, but the size of each expansion varied. Together those additions transformed the original 151 into more than one thousand species.

New Pokémon

Cumulative Growth

The Pokédex grew from 151 to more than 1,000 species

Successive generations steadily expanded the Pokémon universe. By Generation IX, the National Pokédex contained 1,025 species, more than six times the original roster.

Then and Now

The original 151 now make up only 14.7% of the Pokédex

Generation I began with 151 Pokémon. Across nine generations, that original roster has become a relatively small share of the modern Pokédex.

Types & Statistics

Pokémon changed too

Growth did not simply add more of the same. Type composition varied between generations, while the typical total base-stat profile generally shifted upward.

Type Composition

Type composition shifted across generations

Different generations introduced noticeably different mixes of Pokémon types. Darker cells represent a greater number of Pokémon of that type.

Note: Dual-type Pokémon are counted once for each of their types. Types reflect current PokéAPI classifications, so Pokémon that were reclassified later are shown using their current type.

Type Rankings

Type dominance shifts from generation to generation

No single Pokémon type holds the same position across every generation. Water, shown by default, remained near the top for most generations before falling from rank 1 in Generation VIII to rank 6 in Generation IX. Use the dropdown to follow another type.

How to read: Rank 1 means that the selected type was among the most common types introduced in that generation. Choose another type from the dropdown to follow its ranking over time.

Base Statistics

Typical base stats have generally increased in later generations

Pokémon introduced in later generations tend to have higher total base stats, although there is substantial overlap. The median rises from 405 in Generation I to 488.5 in Generation IX.

Total base stats are calculated as the sum of HP, Attack, Defense, Special Attack, Special Defense and Speed. Outlying points represent Pokémon beyond 1.5 times the interquartile range.

Stat Profile

Generation IX Pokémon average higher stats across all six categories

Compared with Generation I, Pokémon introduced in Generation IX have higher average values across all six base-stat categories. The largest increase is in HP, followed by Attack and Defense.

How to read: Each row represents one of the six base stats. Longer connecting lines indicate a larger difference between Generation I and Generation IX.

From Creatures → To a Global Franchise

Pokémon's expansion was not limited to the Pokédex.

The games themselves also changed how they reached players, moving from staggered regional releases toward near-simultaneous worldwide launches.

03
Global Reach

Pokémon went global

Pokémon became increasingly global in two ways. Its fictional regions drew inspiration from locations beyond Japan, while the games themselves moved from staggered international releases towards near-simultaneous worldwide launches.

Release Convergence

International release delays collapsed within five generations

Early Pokémon games reached overseas markets months or even years after Japan. By Generation VI, the major release regions shown here received the games on the same day.

How to read: Each line represents one international release market. The vertical axis shows the number of days between the Japanese release and that market's release.

04
Pokémon in the Real World

Pokémon stepped into the real world

Beyond video games, Pokémon has become part of Japan's public spaces through Poké Lids, specially designed utility-hole covers featuring Pokémon. This dataset records 470 installations across 41 Japanese prefectures.

Poké Lid Locations

470 Poké Lids reveal an uneven footprint across Japan

Each blue dot represents a recorded Poké Lid. The installations extend across Japan, but some areas have considerably more than others. Hover over a point to discover its location and featured Pokémon.

How to read: Each point represents one Poké Lid. Multiple nearby locations may appear clustered. The map reflects the 470 records in the dataset snapshot, rather than necessarily every Poké Lid currently installed in Japan.

Prefecture Comparison

Four prefectures account for over a third of the recorded Poké Lids

The 470 Poké Lids are not evenly distributed across Japan. Hokkaido leads with 50 recorded installations, followed by Fukushima (43), Miyagi (37) and Iwate (36). Together, these four prefectures account for 35.3% of the dataset.

How to read: Each circle represents one prefecture, with its area proportional to the number of recorded Poké Lids. Circles are positioned at a representative recorded Poké Lid location within each prefecture. Prefectures without records in this dataset are not shown with a circle.

Generation I Footprint

The original 151 feature on two-thirds of recorded Poké Lids

Despite making up only 14.7% of the nine-generation Pokédex, Generation I Pokémon feature on 315 of the 470 recorded Poké Lids (67.0%). Their representation varies considerably across Japan. All 50 recorded Poké Lids in Hokkaido feature at least one Generation I Pokémon, compared with just 4 of 31 in Mie Prefecture.

How to read: Darker prefectures have a higher percentage of recorded Poké Lids featuring at least one Generation I Pokémon. Each lid counts once, even if it features multiple Generation I Pokémon. Grey prefectures have no records in this dataset snapshot, while pale blue can represent a genuine 0%. Percentages are based on the 470-record snapshot, not every Poké Lid currently installed in Japan.
