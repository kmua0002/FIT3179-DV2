From 151 to 1000+

How the Pokémon universe evolved across generations

Pokémon has expanded dramatically since Generation I. This visualisation explores how the Pokédex, types, statistics and global reach of the franchise have changed over time.

1. The Pokédex kept growing

Every generation has expanded the Pokédex, but the size of each expansion has varied considerably. Generation V introduced 156 Pokémon, slightly more than the original 151 of Generation I.

Generation V introduced the largest new roster

The Pokédex grew from 151 to more than 1,000 species

Successive generations steadily expanded the Pokémon universe. By Generation IX, the National Pokédex contained 1,025 species, more than six times the original roster.

The original 151 now make up only 14.7% of the Pokédex

Generation I began with 151 Pokémon. Across nine generations, the roster has grown to 1,025 species, meaning the original generation now represents less than one-sixth of the modern Pokédex.

2. Pokémon changed too

Growth did not simply add more of the same. The mix of Pokémon types introduced in each generation has varied considerably, revealing different patterns across the series.

Type composition shifted across generations

Note: Dual-type Pokémon are counted once for each of their types. Types reflect current PokéAPI classifications, so Pokémon that were reclassified later are shown using their current type.

Typical base stats have generally increased in later generations

Pokémon introduced in later generations tend to have higher total base stats, although there is substantial overlap between generations. The median rises from 405 in Generation I to 488.5 in Generation IX.

Total base stats are calculated as the sum of HP, Attack, Defense, Special Attack, Special Defense and Speed. Outlying points represent Pokémon beyond 1.5 times the interquartile range.
